# Sound and music

The underground soundscape uses low air and stone resonance, sparse modal notes, and short material cues. Upper workings are warm and low; fungal chambers have a softer, airy bed; ancient halls resonate lower; crystal caverns use brighter partials; volcanic depths have the lowest rumble. Visible combat brings a restrained pulse and quicker notes, with a seven-second release back to exploration. Warnings and Hearth outcomes sit above ordinary work.

All audio is original procedural synthesis in [audio definitions](src/content/audio.ts) and [the audio service](src/view/audio.ts). There are no third-party samples, recordings or music licenses to track. The authored frequency envelopes, deterministic noise buffer and modal note sequences are the prototype assets; they can later be replaced with recordings behind the same cue IDs. They currently sound deliberately simple and lack performed music, voices and detailed environmental recordings.

The read-only [event adapter](src/game/audio-events.ts) observes productive excavation/reinforcement/crafting/research, distance traveled, resource delivery, claiming, built rooms/bridges, actual combat timestamps, triggered defenses, spells, notifications and victory/defeat. Hounds and spiders bite, goblins/guards clash, heavy creatures grind stone, fungal creatures exhale, crystal creatures ring and cinderlings crackle. No audio callback changes gameplay.

Audio starts after a pointer or keyboard interaction, following [browser audio activation guidance](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Web_Audio_API/Best_practices). Main-menu Settings and the sidebar's ♪ button provide master, music, effects and mute controls. Preferences live only for the session. Sound settings pause the world; closing restores its prior pause state. Muted play retains every notification, sidebar value and visible combat effect.

Sources on undiscovered tiles are suppressed. Local cues attenuate by distance to the camera target and have no precise stereo bearing; critical warnings have no world coordinates. The score reacts only to visible recent attacks. Repeated cues are throttled globally, footsteps require traveled distance and room sounds occur less frequently than tools. At most twelve short voices and two ambience/score-bed sources run concurrently. Definitions own these tunable limits.

Pause, menus, hidden tabs, loading and travel stop current sounds; resuming starts one fresh ambience pair and does not replay old events. World replacement clears event history. Outcome cues play once and stop ambience. The audio service closes its context during hot reload. Visual effects and audio both use simulation attack timestamps, so animation-speed or haste changes cannot move a hit sound away from its actual outcome.
