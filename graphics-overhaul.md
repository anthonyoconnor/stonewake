# Graphics overhaul: characters, terrain and comparison studios

The subsequent [spells, traps and Hearthstones overhaul](arcana-overhaul.md) extends the comparison studios with thirteen more pairs. Its archives capture the later starting state and preserve this pass's existing `*-baseline.ts` files.

This pass follows the user's 2026-09-09 request to revisit every character against its concept sheet, preserve the starting models, then refine terrain and the appearance of the whole stronghold. All six residents and ten enemies have been rebuilt and reviewed individually. Terrain now combines authored surface textures, continuous geological geometry, revised resources and warmer room lighting. Simulation, character activities, both comparison studios, final environment captures, typechecks, build and production smoke verification have passed. The completed verification record is in [development-history.md](development-history.md); current project status belongs in [development-plan.md](development-plan.md).

The first full comparison exposed incorrect triangle winding, open loft ends and filled-in concave blades. Correct outward faces, closed caps and triangulated concave outlines resolved those defects. Subsequent front/rear/three-quarter reviews drove character-specific work: connected dwarf faces, visible hair relief, smoother transported tube frames, organic animal armor, clearer Sentinel facial recesses and restrained Cinderling glow. Final close views prompted averaged seam normals, softer beard/crown/fold transitions, finer finger creases and restrained cloth, leather and brushed-metal surface detail. The enemy back/stance review corrected submerged scutes, extended the Goblin cowl and adjusted foot contact.

Models remain editable Babylon.js geometry. [Resident sculpting](src/view/resident-sculpt.ts), [dwarf models](src/view/dwarf-sculpt.ts), [Stonehands](src/view/stonehands.ts), [Hounds](src/view/hounds.ts), [enemy sculpting](src/view/enemy-sculpt.ts) and [enemy models](src/view/enemy-models.ts) separate construction from the existing animation updates. Existing gameplay state and rig entry points are preserved. Static pieces merge within their material and animated pivot; equipment toggles remain independent. Fewer merged meshes are useful evidence of construction efficiency, not a measured frame-rate claim.

## Terrain and whole-level direction

The approved [single-layer resource terrain](concept-art/terrain/resource-terrain-v2.png) remains the authority for common bank height, walkable floor and recognizable top/side resource treatment. New [cohesion v1](concept-art/terrain/stronghold-cohesion-v1.png) explored weathered warm earth, cool dense rock, worn paving, timber/bronze furnishings and localized warm light. Review found an incorrect fire core, incidental low walls and stepped-looking ground. [Cohesion v2](concept-art/terrain/stronghold-cohesion-v2.png) restores the blue crystalline Hearth and strengthens the continuous geological treatment. Illustrated partitions or apparent height changes do not add room walls or additional terrain layers to gameplay. Both generation prompts and review notes are retained in [the concept record](concept-art/terrain/graphics-overhaul-prompts.md).

The implementation replaces repeated masonry-like natural surfaces and floating wall shelves with one solid cell mesh carrying shallow fractured side relief and a common top plane. Continuous texture coordinates carry geology and paving across adjacent cells. Generated albedo maps and restrained derived normal maps add material detail without raising the floor. Room tints and boundary inlays preserve room identity; the same furnishing and construction systems continue to supply room services. Gold uses broad shallow fragments along irregular embedded veins; gems use uneven grouped blue/violet mineral forms with darker facets. Water uses subdued continuous ripples; lava uses cooled crust and luminous fissures. Visible stove fires and reading lights support the cool Hearth with warm pools while retaining the existing bounded source-light service and discovery rules.

The three runtime surface assets—[fractured earth](public/art/terrain/fractured-earth.png), [slate](public/art/terrain/slate.png) and [flagstone](public/art/terrain/flagstone.png)—were generated for this project with the built-in image-generation tool on 2026-09-09. Their exact prompts and provenance are in [the material asset record](public/art/terrain/README.md); no third-party texture pack was used. The concept images are visual targets, while these separate surface maps are actual runtime assets. [Terrain materials](src/view/terrain-materials.ts), [terrain sculpting](src/view/terrain-sculpt.ts), [hazard surfaces](src/view/hazard-surfaces.ts) and [environment details](src/view/environment.ts) own their rendering.

## Retained comparison studios

Start the local development server and use **Debug → Test harnesses → Additional test scenarios**. These scenarios are kept in the source catalog and can be reloaded throughout later development:

- [Character before/after gallery](http://127.0.0.1:5173/?scenario=graphics-gallery&paused=1): select `graphics-gallery`. Sixteen pairs contain the archived starting models and the current live factories. Amber rings identify starting models; teal rings identify revised models. Select a character or use the arrows, focus a pair, show all sixteen, rotate both with Front/Back/Turn, and zoom. Starting/Refined focuses one member for close inspection; the tiny Stonehand retains its native scale. Choose an Animation to play both versions together, with pause, restart, frame stepping and 0.25×/0.5×/1×/2× speed. Exhibits remain outside the gameplay simulation, with no combat, resource needs or world labels.
- [Terrain comparison studio](http://127.0.0.1:5173/?scenario=terrain-comparison&paused=1): select `terrain-comparison`. The starting half is left and the revised half right in the default view. Choose terrain, rooms, hazards or the whole chamber; Starting/Refined buttons provide matching close views. The region selector rebuilds both halves for each of the five biome palettes. Regular camera pan/orbit/zoom still works.

Use the port printed by Vite if it differs from 5173. Scenario query parameters are development-only. **Return to stronghold** restores the retained game in memory and removes comparison displays; this is not a save system.

Animation options include idle and walking for all sixteen pairs, attacks for fighting types, and each resident's role activity: mining, crafting, training, research or sniffing. The Tunnel Burrower also previews excavation. Both renderers receive the same sampled inputs and timing through [gallery playback](src/view/gallery-playback.ts); the original/current animation code remains authoritative. Walking stays on the plinth. Reduced-motion preferences remain active. Preview playback has its own controls and works while the test world's simulation is paused; menus and background tabs suspend it. Frame stepping advances 1/30 second regardless of speed. Changing models preserves a supported clip and falls back to idle when that action is unavailable.

The old gallery and gameplay minimum camera distances have been removed from the wheel and zoom buttons, and the obsolete minimum setting is gone. The near clipping plane follows close camera distances. The existing maximum distance remains, along with a machine-precision positive-radius guard. Very close views can enter the model geometry; Focus selected pair restores a useful framing.

The terrain chamber contains mirrored geology, claimed/raw floor, all six existing room types with automatic furnishings, water/lava/chasm specimens and stone bridges. Rooms use normal construction validation and costs. Bridge plans use normal validation/pricing, then the fixture invokes the same completion service used by workers to open with finished exhibits. No new gameplay room type or special capacity rule is introduced.

Both studios suppress pointer and local source illumination for an even comparison. Character pairs retain their native world scale and share directional studio lighting, empty cargo and matching book-holding poses. Original model, scene, terrain and surface modules are archived as `*-baseline.ts`. Separate material caches prevent live changes from recoloring those originals, and archived material objects/textures are disposed on exit. Comparison controls and identification stay in the sidebar. The current room/furnishing definitions still feed the terrain fixture; future changes to those definitions should preserve any newly needed baseline data too.

## Practical limits

These are authored procedural models and stylized materials, not pixel-identical reproductions of the concept sheets or imported high-resolution sculpt assets. Shared limb pivots remain; there is no finger articulation, cloth deformation, foot IK or exact furniture-contact solver. Concept microdetail is simplified for the overhead game camera. Derived texture normals approximate fine surface relief, and soft contact treatment does not provide full detailed cast shadows. Readability, consistent proportions and coherent material/light relationships at gameplay distance remain the acceptance criteria alongside close inspection.
